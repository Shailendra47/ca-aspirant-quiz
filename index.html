ICAI Code of Ethics 2020 • Chapter 1

Fundamental Principles & Threats to Independence

Authoritative framework governing integrity, objectivity, competence, safeguards, and ethical threat mitigation.

⏱️ Est. Reading Time: 12 Mins • 📋 Syllabus Code: CA Final Group 1 / Paper 3 •
01

The Five Fundamental Principles of Professional Ethics

The ICAI Code of Ethics (Revised 2020) establishes Five Fundamental Principles that are binding upon every Chartered Accountant in all professional and business assignments:

1 Integrity

A member must be straightforward, honest, and truthful in all professional and business dealings. A member shall not knowingly be associated with reports, returns, or communications containing materially false or misleading statements.

2 Objectivity

A member must not compromise professional or business judgment because of bias, conflict of interest, or undue influence of clients, employers, or third parties.

3 Professional Competence & Due Care

Attain and maintain professional knowledge and skill at the level required to ensure competent professional service; act diligently in accordance with technical and professional standards.

4 Confidentiality

Respect confidentiality of information acquired through professional relationships. Do not disclose such information to third parties without proper and specific authority, unless there is a legal or professional right or duty to disclose.

5 Professional Behaviour

Comply with relevant laws and regulations and avoid any conduct that the member knows or should know might discredit or bring disrepute to the accountancy profession.

02

The Five Threats to Independence (Mnemonic: SAFIS)

Independence of mind and independence in appearance are constantly challenged by circumstances creating one or more of the Five Statutory Threats:

1. Self-Interest Threat (Financial & Material Benefit)

Occurs when a financial or other interest will inappropriately influence the auditor's judgment. Examples: Holding shares in the audit client, excessive dependence on total fee income from a single client (>15% over consecutive years), loan or guarantees from non-banking clients, or fear of losing the engagement.

2. Self-Review Threat (Auditing One's Own Handiwork)

Occurs when a previous judgment needs to be re-evaluated by the member responsible for that judgment. Examples: Preparing original accounting entries, statutory audit of a company where the firm performed valuation or internal audit in the same period, or designing the financial IT system being audited.

3. Advocacy Threat (Becoming the Client's Champion)

Occurs when a member promotes a client's position to the point where objectivity is compromised. Examples: Acting as an advocate for an audit client in litigation before an appellate tax tribunal or court, underwriting or promoting the client's public share issue.

4. Familiarity Threat (Over-Identification & Complacency)

Occurs when, by virtue of a close relationship with an audit client, its directors, or employees, a member becomes too sympathetic to their interests. Examples: Audit partner serving the same client for decades, immediate family member serving as Chief Financial Officer (CFO), accepting expensive gifts or hospitality beyond trivial business courtesies.

5. Intimidation Threat (Pressure & Coercion)

Occurs when a member is deterred from acting objectively with professional skepticism due to actual or perceived pressures. Examples: Being threatened with dismissal or replacement, client threatening personal litigation, pressure to reduce audit hours inappropriately to cut fees.

03

Safeguards & The Ethical Standards Board (ESB) Framework

The Conceptual Framework Approach

The Code mandates a 3-step approach: (1) Identify threats → (2) Evaluate significance → (3) Apply safeguards to eliminate threats or reduce them to an acceptable level. If safeguards cannot reduce threats to an acceptable level, the member must decline or terminate the engagement.

Safeguards Created by Profession & Law
  • Mandatory Continuing Professional Education (CPE).
  • Mandatory Peer Review of audit firms.
  • Statutory rotation of auditors (Companies Act Sec 139).
  • NFRA & ICAI Disciplinary oversight.
Safeguards in Work Environment (SQC 1)
  • Engagement Quality Control Review (EQCR) partner.
  • Rotation of senior engagement personnel.
  • Firm-wide independence declaration before signing.
  • Separate teams for advisory vs audit engagements.
Authoritative ESB Ruling: A member who has drafted the financial statements or books of account cannot accept statutory audit of the same entity for that financial period, regardless of client consent. Independence in appearance is compromised.
ICAI Code of Ethics 2020 • Chapter 2

Auditor Independence, Section 144 & KYC Norms

Section 2(2) Deemed Practice, Section 144 Prohibitions, UDIN Mandates, and PMLA KYC Compliance.

⏱️ Est. Reading Time: 15 Mins • 📋 Statutory Scope: Companies Act 2013 & PMLA •
01

Section 2(2) Deemed Practice & Holding of COP

Under Section 2(2) of the Chartered Accountants Act, 1949, a member is deemed to be in practice when, individually or in partnership with other members, they engage in:

  • Engaging in public practice of accountancy or auditing.
  • Offering to perform or performing services involving audit, verification, or certification of financial accounts.
  • Rendering professional assistance regarding matters of principle or detail relating to accounting procedures or recording of financial data.
  • Rendering other services that Council specifies (tax representation, corporate advisory, secretarial certification).
Crucial Distinction: Even if a member does not operate a formal proprietorship or partnership, if they sign audit reports or represent clients for consideration, they are deemed to be in practice and must hold a valid Certificate of Practice (COP).
02

Section 144 of Companies Act 2013: Prohibited Non-Audit Services

To safeguard statutory auditor independence, Section 144 of the Companies Act, 2013 categorically prohibits a statutory auditor (and their network/associate firms) from providing the following services to the audit client, its holding company, or its subsidiary:

❌ 1. Accounting and book-keeping services
❌ 2. Internal audit services
❌ 3. Design & implementation of financial IT systems
❌ 4. Actuarial services
❌ 5. Investment advisory services
❌ 6. Investment banking services
❌ 7. Outsourced financial services
❌ 8. Management services
⚠️ Strict Application: These prohibitions cannot be waived by the Board of Directors, Audit Committee, or General Meeting resolution. Providing any of these services automatically invalidates statutory auditor appointment.
03

Mandatory KYC Norms & Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO) Thresholds

Under ICAI Council Guidelines read with PMLA Rules, practicing CAs must verify client identity and assess risk classifications:

Client Risk Categorization Review

High Risk Clients: PEPs, offshore accounts, cash-intensive units. Must undergo re-verification at least once every 1 to 2 years.

Medium / Low Risk: Comprehensive review every 3 to 5 years.

UBO Ownership Thresholds

• Company: Controlling interest > 10% shares/capital/profits.

• Partnership Firm: Capital/profit entitlement > 15%.

• Unincorporated Trust: Property interest of 10% or more.

04

UDIN Mandates & Office Sharing Norms

UDIN (Unique Document Identification Number)

Mandatory for all attest functions, audit reports, and tax certificates. Must be generated within a strict window of 60 days from the date of signing. Failure to generate UDIN violates Council Directives and constitutes misconduct under Clause (1) Part II Second Schedule.

Sharing Office Space (Demarcation Norms)

A CA in practice may share office space with other recognized professionals (e.g. Advocates, Company Secretaries) only if there is clear physical demarcation, separate reception, independent name board, and absolute preservation of client confidentiality. Combining signage with real estate or unapproved trades is misconduct.

ICAI Code of Ethics 2020 • Chapter 3

Fees, Tendering, Contingent Fees & Indebtedness

Regulation 192, Minimum Fee Mandates, Predatory Bidding Prohibitions, and Indebtedness Thresholds.

⏱️ Est. Reading Time: 14 Mins • 📋 Statutory Scope: Clause 10 Part I First Schedule •
01

Prohibition of Contingent & Percentage Fees (Clause 10)

Under Clause (10) of Part I of the First Schedule, a member in practice is guilty of professional misconduct if they charge or accept fees which are based on a percentage of profits or which are contingent upon the findings or results of their work.

Authoritative Exceptions Prescribed under Regulation 192:

In the following recognized circumstances, percentage or contingent fees are legally permissible without attracting misconduct:

✅ 1. Receiver / Liquidator: Fee based on percentage of assets realized or distributed.
✅ 2. Co-operative Society Audits: Fee based on percentage of working capital or turnover fixed by Registrar.
✅ 3. Valuer for Direct Taxes: Fee based on percentage of valuation fixed under CBDT rules.
✅ 4. Debt Recovery Advisory: Success fees permitted within specific monetary caps prescribed by Council.
02

ICAI Guidelines for Responding to Tenders (2016)

Under the revised Tendering Guidelines issued by the ICAI Council:

  • Members are permitted to respond to public tenders/RFPs for professional services, subject to compliance with minimum fee norms.
  • Prohibition of Predatory Pricing: Submitting commercial bids that are manifestly inadequate or heavily discounted solely to undercut competing firms undermines audit quality and violates Council Guidelines.
  • Empanelment Canvassing: Engaging lobbyists, paid intermediaries, or touts to canvass for bank statutory branch audits or PSU panels is strictly prohibited under Clause (5) Part I First Schedule.
  • Minimum Audit Fee Recommendation: The Council recommends baseline hourly/daily fee rates classified by city category (Class A, B, and C).
03

Indebtedness, Borrowings & Substantial Fee Dependency

Indebtedness Disqualification

• Companies Act 2013 (Sec 141(3)(d)(ii)): An auditor or their partner/relative indebted to the company/subsidiary > ₹5,00,000 is statutorily disqualified.
• Council Norms: A member in practice shall not borrow from an audit client exceeding ₹1,00,000 (unless borrowed from a commercial bank in ordinary course).

Substantial Fee Dependency (>15% Rule)

Where the total fees generated from an audit client (and its group) exceed 15% of the firm's total gross fee revenue for two consecutive years, a significant Self-Interest threat arises. The firm must disclose this to the governance committee and apply an external pre- or post-issuance quality review.

ICAI Code of Ethics 2020 • Chapter 4

Marketing, Advertising, Publicity & Disciplinary Machinery

Clauses 6 & 7 of First Schedule, Website Guidelines, Press Interviews, and Disciplinary Procedures.

⏱️ Est. Reading Time: 15 Mins • 📋 Statutory Scope: Board of Discipline vs Disciplinary Committee •
01

Solicitation & Advertising Professional Attainments (Clauses 6 & 7)

Clause (6) Part I First Schedule: Soliciting Work

A member in practice shall not solicit clients or professional work, either directly or indirectly, by circular, advertisement, personal communication or interview.

Permitted: Applying for empanelment rosters where advertisements call for applications; responding to direct unsolicited client inquiries.

Clause (7) Part I First Schedule: Advertising Attainments

A member shall not advertise professional attainments or services, nor use any designation other than "Chartered Accountant".

Strictly Prohibited: Printing "Tax Consultant", "GST Specialist", or "Top Valuer" on visiting cards, signboards, or letterheads.

02

ICAI Website Guidelines (Pull vs Push Mode)

Mandatory Website Principles:

Pull Mode Only: The website must operate strictly in pull mode. Information is provided only to viewers who voluntarily seek it. Push technology (pop-ups, bulk emails, unsolicited sponsored banners) is illegal.

✅ Permitted Details: Names, photos (passport size biographical profile), partner qualifications, office addresses, publications, articles.
❌ Prohibited Details: Client names, client logos without regulatory mandate, fee scales, comparative claims ("Largest firm in city").
03

Disciplinary Machinery: Board of Discipline vs Disciplinary Committee

Board of Discipline (Section 21A)

Jurisdiction: Misconduct specified in the First Schedule (less serious procedural breaches, soliciting, advertising, fee sharing).

Maximum Penalties:

  • Reprimand the member.
  • Suspension of COP up to 3 months.
  • Fine up to ₹1,00,000.
Disciplinary Committee (Section 21B)

Jurisdiction: Misconduct specified in the Second Schedule (or combined First & Second Schedules; gross negligence, falsification, moral turpitude).

Maximum Penalties:

  • Reprimand the member.
  • Removal / Suspension for any period or permanently.
  • Fine up to ₹5,00,000.
CA Final AFM • Chapter 5

Financial Risk Management & Quantitative Derivatives

Value at Risk (VaR), Volatility Scaling, Duration, Convexity, IRS Swaps & Immunization.

⏱️ Est. Reading Time: 18 Mins • 📋 Module: CA Final AFM / Faculty: CA Nikhil Monga •
01

Value at Risk (VaR) Formulation & Time-Scaling Traps

Parametric VaR Formulation

VaR = Z × σ × Portfolio Value

Z-Multipliers (1-tailed):
• 90% = 1.28  |  • 95% = 1.645 (or 1.65)
• 99% = 2.33  |  • 99.9% = 3.09

Square Root of Time Rule: VaR(T-day) = VaR(1-day) × √T. Holds only if daily returns are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.). If positive serial correlation (momentum) exists, √T underestimates true loss!

Annualizing Volatility: Days Scaling Trap

Equity Volatility: σ(annual) = σ(daily) × √250 (or √252 trading days).

Money Market / Fixed Income: Uses 365 calendar days for continuous cash accrual.

Classic Exam Trap: Using 365 calendar days for equity volatility erroneously inflates risk by √(365/252) ≈ 20.4% to 20.7%!

02

Duration, Modified Duration & Convexity Adjustment

Modified Duration (Price Sensitivity)

D_mod = D_mac / (1 + y/m)

Linear Price Change: ΔP / P ≈ - D_mod × Δy.

Tricky Point: Multiplying Macaulay Duration directly by Δy without dividing by (1 + y) is a frequent student trap.

Convexity Adjustment (Taylor Series)

ΔP / P ≈ - D_mod(Δy) + 0.5 × Convexity × (Δy)²

Convexity is always positive for option-free bonds. It increases price more when yields fall than it decreases price when yields rise.

Exam Trap: Forgetting the factor of 1/2 in the Taylor expansion double-counts the convexity benefit!

03

Interest Rate Swaps (IRS) & Redington Immunization

Quick Revision Matrix of Risk Mitigation Tools:
• Redington Immunization: Requires (1) PV(A) ≥ PV(L), (2) D(A) = D(L), and (3) Convexity(A) > Convexity(L). Requires dynamic periodic rebalancing.
• Zero-Cost Collar: Long Out-of-the-Money Cap + Short Out-of-the-Money Floor where the premiums paid and received precisely offset to zero.
• Payer Swaption: The option to enter an IRS paying a fixed rate. Hedges future bond issuance against rising benchmark yields.
• Credit Default Swap (CDS): Cash settlement payout = Notional × (100% - Recovery Rate) upon default credit event.
CA Final AFM • Chapter 6

Mutual Funds Valuation, Returns & Performance Evaluation

Net Asset Value (NAV) Core Traps, Sharpe, Treynor, Jensen's Alpha & Fama Net Selectivity.

⏱️ Est. Reading Time: 18 Mins • 🎬 Video Link: CA Nikhil Monga Revision Class •
01

Net Asset Value (NAV) Core Traps & Mechanics

Net Assets Formula & Components

Formula: [MV of Investments + Accrued Income + Cash/Bank] - [Accrued Expenses + Liabilities] / Units.

Accrued Dividends: Include dividends declared on portfolio shares where the record date has passed before the valuation date, even if the cash payment is still in transit.

Classic Exam Trap: Deducting exit load during portfolio NAV calculation is wrong; exit load is a transaction deduction borne by exiting unitholders, not a scheme liability!

Dividend Equalisation Reserve Mechanics

When new investors enter mid-year at cum-dividend NAV, they pay an Equalisation Amount into the Dividend Equalisation Reserve. This prevents dilution of existing unitholders' accrued distributable income per unit upon dividend declaration.

02

HPR, Annualized Yield, DRIP & CAGR Reverse Working

Holding Period Return (HPR) vs Yield

HPR: [NAV₁ - NAV₀ + Dividend + Capital Gains] / NAV₀.

Annualized Simple Return: HPR × (365 / Number of Days).

CAGR: (Terminal Wealth / Initial Investment)^(365 / Days) - 1.

Reverse Working Trap: When reverse-calculating the holding period in days from annualized return, set: Days = [HPR × 365] / Annualized Yield.

DRIP, Bonus Units & Expense Ratio Drag

DRIP (Reinvestment): New Units = Total Dividend / Ex-Dividend NAV. Expanded units compound future returns.

Bonus Units (e.g. 1:5): New NAV = [Old NAV × 5] / 6, preserving total initial value.

Expense Drag: Regular plan commission drag of 1.0%–1.5% reduces terminal wealth by 35%–45% over 20-year horizons!

03

Advanced Risk-Adjusted Evaluation Metrics (Formulas)

Sharpe vs Treynor Ratios

Sharpe: (R_p - R_f) / σ_p (Uses Total Risk; benchmark for undiversified portfolios).

Treynor: (R_p - R_f) / β_p (Uses Systematic Risk; benchmark for diversified portfolios).

Jensen's Alpha & Fama Net Selectivity

Jensen's Alpha: α_p = R_p - [R_f + β_p(R_m - R_f)] (Stock selection outperformance).

Fama Net Selectivity: α_p - [(σ_p / σ_m) - β_p](R_m - R_f) (Alpha minus cost of diversification).

4. Institutional & SEBI Regulations Highlights
• Close-Ended Arbitrage: Buying at secondary market discounts locks in excess return as price converges to NAV on maturity.
• Side-Pocketing (Segregated Portfolios): Isolates defaulted debt instruments (rated D) into a separate portfolio, preventing liquidity runs.
• High-Water Mark Principle: 20% performance fee is charged only when NAV surpasses previous historical peak.
• Rupee-Cost Averaging (SIP): Fixed monthly outlays ensure average acquisition cost is strictly lower than arithmetic mean NAV.
